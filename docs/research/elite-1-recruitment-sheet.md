# ELITE-1 recruitment sheet (owner)

One page for Saleem. Everything needed to fill 10 seats and nothing else. The
full instrument is `docs/research/elite-1-instrument-en.md` and
`docs/research/elite-1-instrument-ar.md`; this sheet does not repeat it, and the
facilitator reads the instrument rather than this file.

## Authorisation, and three corrections

**Two further corrections were applied on 2026-08-02**, both required by Codex when
it reviewed `d34ebfa`, and both recorded here so a reader of an earlier copy can
see what changed.

1. **The assistive-technology scope was overstated by implication.** The section
   "The assistive-technology round is separate, and it is required" justified A1's
   public-path-only scope by saying that 22 accessibility findings in the private
   flows are recorded and that sending a participant into known defects tests the
   participant rather than the product. Those 22 findings are fixed and awaiting
   independent verification. They are not known-broken surfaces. That section now
   states exactly what A1 validates and what it does not, and schedules a separate
   authenticated assistive-technology session, ELITE-1-AT-B, for the 22.
2. **Raw-notes retention was unbounded.** The section "What is retained, and by
   whom" said notes are kept for as long as the findings they support are open,
   which has no end date. It now sets a bounded period: raw notes are destroyed no
   later than 90 days after the final session, after which only synthesized
   findings without participant identifiers remain. Any different duration is an
   owner or counsel decision recorded in `docs/decision-register.md`.

The same two corrections are applied at parity in both instruments.

**Nobody is contacted yet.** Preparation is authorised. External outreach is not.
Those are two different permissions and this sheet previously ran them together:
it read "Codex has authorised preparation and recruitment", which was wrong, and
wrong in the direction that gets somebody messaged. Codex's condition is stated
plainly: prepare the criteria, the screener, the consent language, the outreach
copy, the scheduling process and the research script, and contact nobody until
the owner separately approves outreach.

So everything in this file and in both instruments is ready to use and none of it
has been used. No message has been sent, no candidate list exists in this
repository, no calendar has been opened and no test account has been created on

**What releases it.** One sentence from the owner authorising outreach for

counsel item stands between that sentence and the first screener call. The
decision date carried in the ledger is 1 October 2026, and bringing it forward

The round is measured from consented research notes and manual observation. No

## Exclusion criteria

Do not invite, and disqualify at screening: anyone employed by, invested in or
advising SAT; anyone whose only real-estate experience is residential; anyone who
cannot describe one specific property or one specific search in the screener,
because every task depends on having a real frame of reference to compare
against; anyone whose participation requires a signed confidentiality agreement,
until the owner has approved one; and anyone whose commercial relationship with
Saleem would make an honest negative answer awkward, which is a judgement call
and is worth making strictly, because a polite session produces nothing.

## Time commitment

45 minutes for the session itself, on a video call, screen shared. Plus a
5 minute screener call or message beforehand. Nothing to prepare, nothing to read
in advance, no follow-up obligation. Tell people 45 minutes and hold to it; a
session that overruns teaches the next person's calendar to decline.

A1, the assistive-technology session, is 45 minutes on the same terms, but its
scope is the public path rather than the full 15 tasks, so it is a shorter list
of tasks in the same amount of time. Do not compress it: a screen-reader
participant working through a page the facilitator can read at a glance needs the
time back, not taken away.

Owner time, for planning: about 13 hours across the round, being 11 sessions of
45 minutes plus scheduling, plus write-up between sessions.

Notes only does not reduce that figure, it moves it. Under a recorded round, part
of the write-up could be deferred to a replay; without a recording the write-up
has to happen while the session is fresh, which is why the scheduling rule below
holds sessions 90 minutes apart and caps the day at three. A second notetaker on
the call is optional and helps, and is bound by every rule in this section: no
recording, no external AI transcription, no names in the notes.

## Language

Ask, do not assume. Screener question 5 is the one that decides it: which

session. The session runs entirely in the chosen language and the product runs in
the same one, right to left throughout for an Arabic session. Seats S1, S2, D1
and D2 are held for Arabic so the round cannot quietly become an English round.

## What each participant needs

Seven of the ten sessions run on a laptop or desktop with a current browser, a
working microphone, and the ability to share the screen on the call. Three run on
a physical handset, and which three is decided before the round rather than by
whoever happens to join from a phone. See "Device coverage" below. The session
runs on the deployed preview, which is not indexed and carries the preview banner.
Send the preview link only at the start of the session, not with the invitation.

Supply side, S1 to S5: a test account on the preview, named `partner-s1` to
`partner-s5`, belonging to a fictional organisation created for the round. Two of
the five register live during the session as task S1; decide which two before the
round and record it, because a participant who registers live and one who starts

Demand side, D1 to D5: a test account named `partner-d1` to `partner-d5`. No

## Device coverage, which is not left to chance

This sheet previously said that a mobile session was worth more than a cancelled
one and that the observation sheet had a device column for exactly that reason.
That is coverage by accident. A round can run all ten sessions on a desktop, tick
every minimum, and close without one piece of evidence about the surface most of
this market actually uses. Codex refused that, and it was right to: mobile is not
a variant of the desktop result, it is a different result.

So three of the ten seats are mobile seats, allocated before outreach, and the
allocation is part of the seat rather than a note about how somebody happened to
join.

| Mobile seat | Seat it occupies | Device and browser | Language | Substitutable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | D1 | iPhone, Safari | Arabic | No |
| M2 | D5 | Android, Chrome | Either | Yes, to another demand seat |
| M3 | S3 | Either handset, whichever the participant carries | Either | Yes, to another supply seat |

M1 is not substitutable and the reason is specific. Arabic on a small screen is
where right-to-left layout, Arabic line breaking, the font stack and touch target
spacing all fail at once if they fail at all, and no desktop Arabic session and
no English mobile session tests that combination. If the person holding seat D1
turns out to be a desktop-only participant, the seat moves to another Arabic
demand candidate rather than the mobile requirement moving off it.

**A physical handset, not a resized window.** A desktop browser narrowed to 390
pixels is a layout check, not a device session. It has a mouse pointer, a
hardware keyboard, no on-screen keyboard covering half the viewport, no address
bar that grows and shrinks as the page scrolls, no thumb reach, no iOS Safari
form behaviour and no real network. Every one of those has produced a defect in
this product's own accessibility work. The reflow and responsive probes already
cover the resized-window case at 320, 360, 390 and 430 pixels and they run on
every package; the mobile sessions exist to cover what those probes cannot see.

**How a handset session is observed.** The participant shares their phone screen
through the call platform, which iOS and Android both support. If screen sharing
from the handset fails, the fallback is the participant joining the call on their
laptop for audio and pointing the laptop camera at the phone. That is worse and
it is still a session. What is not acceptable is the facilitator driving while the
participant describes, because the thing being tested is what their hands do.

**The tasks do not change.** A mobile seat runs the same task scripts as its
desktop equivalent, as far as each task is possible. A task that cannot be
attempted on a handset is recorded as not attempted, with the reason, and that
record is a finding rather than a gap in the round. "The listing studio could not
be completed on a phone" is one of the most useful sentences this round could
produce, and it only exists if somebody tries.

**The gate.** The round does not close with fewer than 3 mobile sessions, and it
does not close without M1. If M1 cannot be filled, the round stays open on that
seat and the shortfall is recorded against the round rather than absorbed into
it.

## The assistive-technology round is separate, and it is required

Codex asked for at least one assistive-technology or screen-reader validation
session, or an explicit statement that this is a separate required round. It is a
separate required round. It is called ELITE-1-AT, it is a minimum of one session,
and claiming stage E1 is gated on it having run.

It is separate rather than an eleventh seat for three reasons. The recruiting
channel is different, because a daily screen-reader user is not found in the same
places as a leasing administrator and is not screened on the same questions. The
scope is different, because the tasks below are a subset chosen to be attemptable
rather than the full 15. And the failure mode is different: an ELITE-1 session
that goes badly produces a usability finding, while an AT session that goes badly
may produce a finding that no unaided participant could have surfaced at all, and
mixing the two lets the second get averaged into the first.

| Seat | Who | Technology | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | A daily screen-reader user, using their own configuration and their own speech rate | VoiceOver on iOS Safari, or NVDA on Windows Chrome | Public path only |

Public path only means: the home page, the listings index with one filter
applied, one listing detail page including its Evidence Passport, and the
requirement form up to but not through submission.

**What A1 validates, stated exactly.** A1 validates that a daily screen-reader
user, on their own configuration and their own speech rate, can operate those four
public surfaces: reach and understand the home page, apply one filter on the
listings index and know what changed, read a listing detail page including its
Evidence Passport and tell a verified fact from an unverified one, and fill the
requirement form up to the point of submission. In journey terms that is journey
1, Find and enquire, up to the enquiry, and the first screen of journey 2, Post a
space requirement.

**What A1 does not validate.** Registration, the Listing Studio, the dashboard,
the deal workspace and every research surface are outside A1's scope, so A1 says
nothing about journeys 3, 4, 5 or 6. In particular, **A1 does not verify the 22
accessibility findings recorded in the private flows.** Those findings are fixed
and awaiting independent verification. They are not known-broken surfaces and must
not be described as such, here or anywhere else. A1 is scoped to the public path
because the public path is what an unauthenticated participant can reach without
test-account credentials and an observed session inside their own account, not
because the private flows are expected to fail.

**The authenticated round is separate, and it is also required.** Verifying the 22
requires a second assistive-technology session, called ELITE-1-AT-B, run against
authenticated surfaces with a prepared test account, seeded inventory and explicit
consent to operate an account under observation. It covers registration, the
Listing Studio and the dashboard, which are the surfaces the 22 findings sit in.
It is not an extension of A1's slot and is not booked in the same week. It is
scheduled after the ELITE-1 round's write-up, because a defect the round surfaces
in those same flows should be fixed before an assistive-technology participant
meets it. Until ELITE-1-AT-B has run and its results are recorded, no document may
state that the 22 findings are closed. They remain fixed and unverified.

The exclusion criteria in this sheet apply to A1 with one change: A1 does not
need commercial real-estate experience and is not screened on a specific property
or a specific search, because A1 is not being asked to judge the market content.
A1 is asked whether the page can be operated at all.

Compensation for A1 is an owner decision and this sheet does not presume it. The
observation is only that expert assistive-technology participants are commonly
paid for their time in a way that a peer in the same industry is not, and that a
round which pays nobody and a round which pays A1 are both defensible while
deciding it after the invitation is not.

A1 is notes only, on the same terms as the rest of the round, and for one
additional reason. A screen reader's speech output is a recording of the
participant's own assistive configuration: their voice choice, their speech rate,
their verbosity settings and their navigation habits. That is personal data about
a disability-related setup, and it is not captured here at all.

## Scheduling

Offer a 45 minute slot and book 60, so the session is never rushed by the next
meeting. Space sessions at least 90 minutes apart, and run no more than 3 in a
day, because the write-up between sessions is where the round's value is
recorded and a facilitator on their fourth session stops noticing things.

Run the two supply-side Arabic sessions early, S1 and S2, because they exercise
registration and Arabic direction together, which is where the round is most
likely to hit something that needs fixing before the remaining 8 people see it.

Before the first session, confirm the preview data checks in section 5 of the
instrument: at least 20 published listings with at least 8 in Riyadh, at least 4
with more than one photograph, at least 2 with a real Evidence Passport record,
at least 1 with a visible gap, `/rent-index` loading with its REGA Rental Index
(Ejar) attribution in both languages, and `/requirements` with at least 2
published requirements. If a check fails, fix it before the round rather than
explaining it during a session.

Reconfirm 24 hours ahead. Expect 2 or 3 no-shows across 10 seats and over-invite
accordingly rather than compressing the mix later.

## Consent steps, and the recording position

**Round one is notes only. No audio, no video, no screen capture, on any of the
eleven sessions including A1.**

This sheet and both instruments previously asked each participant for recording
permission, and this sheet then stated that written consent was unnecessary
because nothing personal was retained. Those two sentences cannot both stand. A
voice recording is personal data, a screen recording of somebody's own phone is
personal data, and a round cannot hold a recording and simultaneously claim it
retains nothing personal. Codex named the contradiction and offered two ways out:
define the whole data-protection position for a recorded round, or make round one
notes only.

Round one is notes only, and the reason is not squeamishness. Defining the
position properly means naming a storage location, a retention date, a named
access list, a deletion method and a lawful processor, and the owner has not made
those arrangements. `docs/status-ledger.md` section 7 records the enterprise AI
agreement and processing terms as not signed, so at present there is no approved
processor a recording could lawfully be sent to. Rewording the claim while
keeping the recording would have removed the contradiction from the page and left
it in the round. Removing the recording removes it from both.

**No external AI transcription, and this is not a formality.** Because there is no
recording, there is nothing to upload; the rule still needs stating because the
easiest way to break it is passively. Turn off the call platform's automatic
transcription, meeting summary and AI notetaker before every session, not after
it starts, because several platforms default them on and a transcript generated
by default is still a recording sent to a processor nobody approved. The
facilitator's typed notes are also not to be pasted into any transcription or
summarisation service the owner has not recorded. Whether a later round may
record, and on what terms, is decision O19 in `docs/decision-register.md` and it
is the owner's to make.

Four moments, in order, none of them skippable.

At invitation: state plainly that there is nothing to buy, sign or agree to, that
nothing confidential is needed, and that the call is not recorded. Nobody should
arrive surprised by any of it, and nobody should arrive expecting to be asked for
recording permission that will not now be sought.

At the start of the session: read section 3 of the instrument aloud, without
paraphrasing the confidentiality paragraph. Say that the session is not recorded
and that the facilitator will be typing throughout, so that a pause and the sound
of a keyboard read as notetaking rather than as disapproval. Attending is not
consent to being quoted by name, and the notes carry no name.

During the session: if a participant volunteers something confidential, an actual
rent, a tenant name, an expansion plan or a contract date, do not write it down,
do not enter it into the product, and do not repeat it in the findings. Note only
that a confidential detail was mentioned, and continue.

At the close: read the closing script. Confirm again that nothing was recorded,
say what the notes hold and where they live, and ask whether there is anything
they would rather not have used. Honour that without asking why, and strike the
row rather than marking it.

## What is retained, and by whom

The research record is pseudonymous, not anonymous, and the difference matters
enough to state. Each row holds side, role group, city, portfolio or search
scope, language, prior platform use and device, and no name, employer, phone
number or email. That is not the same as unidentifiable. A round of ten in a
market this size, described as a portfolio manager of more than three assets in
one named city working in Arabic, is a short list to anyone who knows the market.
So the record is written as though somebody could work out who said it, and
nothing is written into it that would embarrass the person if they did.

The recruitment list, which does hold real names and real contact details, stays
with the owner, on the owner's own device, and never enters this repository. No
commit in this repository may carry a participant's name, employer, phone number
or email, and the seat tracker above is built so that following it makes that
impossible rather than merely discouraged.

**Raw notes are kept for a bounded period, and the bound is a date rather than a
condition.** The previous wording here said the notes are kept for as long as the
findings they support are open. That is not a retention period. A finding can stay
open indefinitely, so a retention rule tied to it is a rule with no end, and a
pseudonymous record of ten identifiable people in a small market must not be held
on an open-ended basis.

The rule is this. Raw session notes, meaning the per-participant record including
their side, role group, city, portfolio or search scope, language, prior platform
use, device and their own words, are retained for a **maximum of 90 days after the
final session of the round**. On or before day 90 the raw notes are destroyed and
only the synthesized findings are kept. The synthesized findings carry no
participant identifier of any kind, including seat labels such as S1 or D3, no
per-participant attribution and no verbatim quotation that could be traced to one
person. They are retained without limit, because at that point they are statements
about the product rather than about anybody.

A finding that is still open on day 91 is carried by its synthesized form. If a
synthesized finding is not specific enough to act on without the raw note behind
it, that is a signal to write the synthesis better before day 90, not a reason to
keep the raw note longer.

A participant may ask for their row to be struck at any point up to the round's
write-up, in which case it is struck rather than marked as withdrawn.

**Any duration other than 90 days is an owner or counsel decision and is recorded
as one** in `docs/decision-register.md` before the round runs, not after. This
sheet does not presume the owner will choose 90 days; it states 90 days as the
recommended maximum so that the round has a lawful default if no other decision is
made. Written consent forms, and any retention beyond what is set out here, are an
O5 counsel item. Their absence does not block the round from running on the spoken
script; their presence would be required before any round that records.

## The order of operations, once outreach is authorised

Not a plan for the round. The round is the instrument's job. This is only the
sequence for the first week, because the ordering is where a round of ten quietly
becomes a round of six.

1. Run the six data checks in instrument section 5 first, before any invitation.
   A check that fails is a fix, and a fix is cheaper before ten people have a slot
   in their calendar than after.
2. Create the ten test accounts and the fictional organisation. Decide and write
   down which two of S1 to S5 register live during their session, because a
   participant who registers live and one who starts signed in are not running the
   same task.
3. Invite in one batch, not in order of seat. Over-invite: expect two or three
   no-shows across ten seats.
4. Screen every reply with the six questions before offering a slot. Stop at the
   first disqualifying answer. A seat filled by somebody who could not describe a
   specific case in question 2 is worse than an empty seat, because the round then
   carries an answer nobody can trace to a real frame of reference.
5. Book S1 and S2 first. They exercise registration and Arabic direction together,
   which is where the round is most likely to find something worth fixing before
   the other eight see it.
6. Write up between sessions, not at the end of the day.
